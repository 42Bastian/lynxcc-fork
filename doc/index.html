Atari Lynx Game Development SDK

lynxcc is a complete game development SDK for the Atari Lynx — a full C toolchain and runtime for building cartridge games end to end. It bundles a Lynx-tuned C compiler, macro assembler, linker and archiver alongside a runtime library with direct-call graphics, joystick and serial drivers, Suzy hardware-math operators, sprite and audio tooling, and a .lnx cartridge builder — all wired together with ready-to-build examples.

About the Atari Lynx

The Atari Lynx was the world’s first full-colour portable video game system, released in 1989 (US) and 1990 (UK). Designed at Epyx and brought to market by Atari Corporation, it was sold from 1989 to 1995, with around 3 million units shipped. Its two custom CMOS chips — Suzy (a sprite-blitting and math co-processor) and Mikey (the CPU host, video DMA and sound engine) — gave it hardware sprite scaling, distortion and clipping, a four-channel DAC and a back-lit colour LCD, well ahead of its handheld contemporaries. The whole lynxcc SDK exists to target exactly this hardware.

Atari Lynx hardware specifications
DeveloperEpyx; brought to market by Atari Corporation
Retail availability1989–1995; around 3 million units sold
Display3.5-inch back-lit colour LCD (backlight on/off switch added on the Lynx II); 160 × 102 resolution; 4096-colour (12-bit) palette, 16 simultaneous colours per scanline. The original screen can be swapped for a modern LCD mod kit, greatly improving picture quality.
Audio4 channels, 8-bit DAC per channel (a 32-bit DAC overall), roughly 100 Hz to above 20 kHz. Lynx I is mono; Lynx II adds stereo with panning. Speaker: 8 Ω 0.2 W (Lynx I), 16 Ω 0.2 W (Lynx II).
CPU8-bit 65SC02 (a MOS 6502 derivative) running up to 4 MHz (~3.6 MHz average). Later Lynx II units switched to a 65C02.
Suzy (16-bit @ 16 MHz)Blitter sprite engine with collision detection (unlimited sprites in principle, ~128 in practice); hardware sprite scaling, distortion and tilting; hardware decoding of compressed sprite data; hardware clipping and multi-directional scrolling; math co-processor. Variable frame rate up to 75 fps.
Mikey (16-bit @ 16 MHz)Hosts the CPU, drives the four-channel 8-bit-per-channel sound engine (stereo with panning on the Lynx II), and runs the video DMA driver feeding the 160 × 102, 4096-colour LCD.
Memory & storage512 bytes of bootstrap / cartridge-loading ROM; 64 KB of 120 ns DRAM; cartridges of 128, 256 and 512 KB (up to 2 MB with bank switching). SD/flash-card cartridges are available that can load many official and homebrew ROMs.
NetworkComLynx serial UART supporting up to 18-player connectivity.
Ports3.5 mm stereo headphone jack (mono on Lynx I); ComLynx network port; 9 V power.
Power9 V DC supply, or 6× AA batteries giving 4–5 hours (Lynx I) or 5–6 hours (Lynx II) of play.
